Sealed results gallery

Six signed bundles. Five shapes of result. One record.

Every card below is the same record, smrforge.result.v1 inside an Evidence Envelope, signed with the SMRFORGE production key. Download the raw JSON, verify it offline by recomputing the SHA-256 and checking the Ed25519 signature, then re-derive it by running the open engine on the inputs sealed inside it. The seal proves custody of the computation. It does not prove the physics is right.

1 · integrity, offline

$ smrforge verify <bundle.json>

Recomputes every SHA-256 in the manifest and checks the Ed25519 signature over the bundle hash. No physics is imported. The in-browser version does the same with no crypto library behind it.

2 · re-derive the number

$ smrforge reproduce <bundle.json>

Re-runs the open engine on the bundle’s own sealed inputs. Deterministic cards re-derive bit-identical. Monte Carlo cards carry a sealed tolerance, a re-run within 3σ.

3 · origin, decided by you

key ed25519:cfb88804…d69b52c

Origin is never a field in the bundle. Your verifier compares the signing key to the pinned production key in the trust root, and decides.

Every grade on this page was written into the bundle by the engine at mint time and is covered by the signature, so a reviewer who changes screening to reference breaks the seal the same way as changing the number. A sealed number from a badly specified core is still a faithfully preserved wrong answer. Nobody should license a reactor on a SMRFORGE number alone.

The seal proves custody of the computation, whatever its shape. k_eff is the demo, not the limit.

pcm = per cent mille (10−5 Δk/k). w/o = weight percent U-235. C/E = computed over experiment. Verification (vs analytic and published references) is not validation (vs measured experiments). Nuclear data cited on this page is openly distributable (ENDF/B-VIII.1).
