The problem

AI produces numbers faster than anyone can defend them.

The person who acts on a number is the one who has to defend it later: an engineer signing a design, an auditor signing off, a regulator deciding whether to believe it. For that person, "the model produced it" is not an answer. They need to open the number up and re-derive it. In nuclear this is the whole bottleneck, where every safety-relevant number is defended to the NRC by hand, for months.

How it complements your AI

It sits alongside your AI, not in front of it.

The model's reasoning stays a black box. The result it lands on does not have to.

The proof layer

SMRFORGE adds a defensibility layer to what you deliver. It does not replace any of it.

When an agent's work ends in a real calculation, that calculation can be sealed, independently re-run, and signed, so the engineer or regulator downstream can confirm it cold. It is the auditability, traceability, and reproducibility layer that sits alongside a production AI system.

Check it yourself

The three moves a reviewer makes.

No trust in us required. Verify, reproduce, redact.

verify

Confirm the seal offline

A standalone checker that imports nothing from our engine. A pass is a real second opinion, not us grading our own homework.

reproduce

Re-run the open engine

Re-derive the number from the sealed inputs. This is what a signed PDF can't do: it proves the number is real, not just unaltered.

redact

Seal without exposing

Strip a customer's inputs from a public copy while the seal still verifies. Provably sealed and untampered, inputs never shown.
